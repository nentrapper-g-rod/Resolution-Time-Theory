# Time Resolution Theory — Original vision in plain language

**Archival record.** This explains the **original** TRT vision as presented in Version 2.4 (2025).  
It is **not** a statement of validated laboratory results, and it is **not** the later, narrowed “measurement-record” guide used in the live repository (`docs/LAYMANS_GUIDE.md`).

**Source:** founding paper Version 2.4 (see `TRT_v2.4_original.pdf`).

## The big idea

Everyday devices need a shared clock. Two modems sending perfect data with **no common timing reference** do not produce a clean conversation — they produce noise.

The original TRT claim is that the universe is the same kind of system. There must be a single, shared **Uniform Time** (a master clock) that everything is locked to. What we call quantum randomness is largely what finite, slow measuring devices see when they try to read a fast, ordered signal that is timed to that cosmic clock.

> If the universe has no master clock, it is just noise.

---

## Time vs. time resolution

**Time** (in this picture) is the underlying, uniform tick of the cosmos.  
**Time resolution** is how finely a given observer or detector can resolve events along that tick.

A slow camera shutter turns a spinning propeller into a transparent disk. The disk is real *as a photograph*. The propeller is not really a disk. TRT’s original claim is that many “quantum” appearances are the photograph, not the propeller.

---

## Founding pictures (metaphors, not experiments)

1. **Slow shutter** — Fast motion blurs into streaks or continuous bands when the exposure is long.
2. **Hummingbird at 30 frames per second** — Wings look like a haze; raise the frame rate and the path appears.
3. **Aliasing** — Sample a high-frequency wave too slowly and you invent a false slow wave.
4. **Strobe wheel** — Flash timing can make a wheel look stopped or running backward.
5. **Poker-machine PRNG** — Outputs look random until you know the seed and the algorithm; the pattern was there all along.
6. **Modem without a shared clock** — Structured bits become static when sender and receiver are not locked to the same timing reference.

## What Version 2.4 set out to cover

The original paper was broad. In plain terms it argued that:

- **Mass** can be read as energy we have not resolved in time (a “frozen” residual tied to limited temporal resolution).
- **Observation** depends on a resolution window Δt: what you count depends on how long you integrate.
- **Superposition and interference** can be pictured as under-sampling of definite, fast processes — the blur of the slow shutter.
- **Bell-type correlations** might be approached with a shared timing reference (a strong assumption, stated as part of the original program).
- **Relativity’s “time dilation”** might be rephrased as changes in resolution with speed and gravity, not as changes in a master Uniform Time itself.

The closing slogan of that vision:

> Time never dilates. Resolution does.
