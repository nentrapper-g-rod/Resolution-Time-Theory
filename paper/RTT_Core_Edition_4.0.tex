\begin{abstract}
Resolution Time Theory (RTT) is proposed as a candidate classical mechanism whose coarse-grained statistics recover aspects of quantum equilibrium and which yields one potentially discriminating experimental signature. This edition is organized as a research program rather than a replacement for quantum mechanics.

A classical high-frequency field produces intensity $I$ by interference. Kapitza averaging and multiplicative noise supply forces proportional to $\nabla I$ and diffusion proportional to $I$. Neither produces a drift proportional to $\nabla\log I$. That obstruction is stated precisely: Born-rule equilibrium for this ontology does not follow from ponderomotive dynamics alone. RTT therefore introduces an explicit equilibrium postulate, $V_{\rm eff}=-\kappa\log I$, which yields an It\^o process with stationary density $\rho_\infty=I$ exactly. A geometric identification $\tau_c\sim\Delta L/v$ links the resolution timescale to interferometer path difference and beam velocity. Falsification conditions are stated. Open problems define the remaining program.
\end{abstract}

\section{Established: the obstruction $\nabla I$ versus $\nabla\log I$}

A classical high-frequency field averages to intensity $I$. Standard results give:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Ponderomotive / Kapitza force: $F\propto\nabla I$.
\item Multiplicative noise tracking $|\Phi|$: effective $D\propto I$.
\item It\^o / Stratonovich conversion for $D\propto I$: drift $\propto\nabla I$, not $\nabla\log I$.
\end{enumerate}
The Fokker--Planck equation with $F\propto\nabla I$ and $D\propto I$ does \emph{not} have stationary density $\rho\propto I$. That requires a drift $D\nabla\log I$. This is the obstruction.

\section{Postulate: equilibrium potential}

RTT adopts $V_{\rm eff}=-\kappa\log I$. Then
\[
\mathrm{d}X=D\,\partial_x\log I_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2D}\,\mathrm{d}W
\]
has $\rho_\infty=I_\varepsilon$ exactly by the Fokker--Planck theorem. Numerics converge as $L^1\sim N^{-1/2}$. The postulate is not derived from Kapitza averaging; deriving it from the field remains open.

\section{Geometric $\tau_c$ and experimental comparison}

\[
\tau_c\sim\frac{\Delta L}{v}.
\]
For electrons at $100\,\mathrm{eV}$: $\Delta L=0.1\,\mu\mathrm{m}\Rightarrow\tau_c\approx17\,\mathrm{fs}$; $\Delta L=1\,\mu\mathrm{m}\Rightarrow\tau_c\approx170\,\mathrm{fs}$. The quantum phase time $\tau_\phi\sim\hbar/\delta E$ is typically a few femtoseconds. Visibility versus gate width under RTT and under standard phase averaging can differ when $\tau_c\neq\tau_\phi$; both timescales are apparatus-defined.

\section{Falsification}

RTT would be strained if gated visibility follows pure quantum phase averaging where the geometric kernel predicts a clear deviation (after decoherence is controlled), or if no resolution timescale of order $\Delta L/v$ appears in the data.

\section{Postulates and open problems}

\textbf{Postulates:} $V_{\rm eff}=-\kappa\log I$; master clock; outcome coordination on the preferred foliation.

\textbf{Open:} derive the logarithmic potential from the field; derive Nelson free dispersion from the field; no-signaling / Lorentz constraints; quantitative relativity map.

\section{Conclusion}

RTT is proposed as a candidate classical mechanism. The main technical contribution is a precise statement of the $\nabla I$ versus $\nabla\log I$ obstruction, an explicit equilibrium postulate, and a geometric resolution timescale for a pulsed-gate visibility comparison.
